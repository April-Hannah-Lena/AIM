# 118. Full-order resonance growth for every nonzero real compact potential

**Area:** Wave scattering by a localized medium

**Status:** 🔵 OPEN

**Last checked:** 2026-09-08

## Problem statement

For every odd integer $`d\ge3`$ and every real nonzero $`V\in L^\infty(\mathbb R^d)`$ of compact support, let $`N_V(r)`$ count, with algebraic multiplicity, the poles $`z`$ with $`|z|\le r`$ of the meromorphic continuation of

```math
R_V(z)=(-\Delta+V-z^2)^{-1}:L^2_{\rm comp}(\mathbb R^d)\longrightarrow L^2_{\rm loc}(\mathbb R^d).
```

Prove or disprove that there are $`c_V>0`$ and $`r_V<\infty`$ with $`N_V(r)\ge c_Vr^d`$ for every $`r\ge r_V`$. Nonzero means not zero almost everywhere.

## Application

This predicts a minimum abundance of resonant frequencies generated by any genuine localized real scattering potential, independently of its detailed shape or sign changes.

## References

1. M. Zworski, [Mathematical study of scattering resonances](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13373-017-0099-4), Bulletin of Mathematical Sciences 7 (2017), §2.7, Conjecture 1: full-order lower bound for real potentials.
2. T. Cunningham, [Schroedinger operators with generic potentials achieve maximal resonance density](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09358), preprint (2026), abstract and main theorem: generic-potential resonance density.

## Status review

**Literature check:** Open in cited literature; no later resolution located.

The June 2026 theorem establishes a generic statement and does not cover every nonzero real compactly supported potential. The real-valued assumption excludes known complex-potential phenomena with radically fewer resonances. Counting poles in the whole disk is essential; this is not a narrow-strip counting claim.

**Search audit:** “nonzero real compactly supported potential resonance counting lower bound conjecture”; “Cunningham generic maximal resonance density 2026”. Searches included proof, counterexample, and 2025–2026 updates. No later resolution of the stated problem was located; this is a literature review, not a proof of openness.
