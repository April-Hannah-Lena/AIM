# 275 — Are all totally integrable outer-length billiards ellipses?

**Area:** Geometric dynamics / optimal circumscribed paths

## Problem statement

Let $K\subset\mathbb R^2$ be a compact convex body whose smooth boundary has positive curvature. Define its outer-length billiard map $T$ on $\mathbb R^2\setminus K$ as follows. From $P$ draw the two support lines $\ell_1,\ell_2$, labeling them so that travel from $K$ toward $P$ along $\ell_1$ keeps $K$ on the left. Let $C$ be the exterior circle tangent to both lines and tangent to $\ell_2$ at its contact with $K$. If $\ell_3$ is the third common support line of $C$ and $K$, put $T(P)=\ell_2\cap\ell_3$.

If the entire exterior is foliated by continuous $T$-invariant simple closed curves surrounding $K$, must $\partial K$ be an ellipse?

## Applied significance

This dynamical system encodes stationary perimeter problems for polygons circumscribed about a convex object. Rigidity would identify which objects permit a complete family of such organized paths.

## Status review

**Status:** Open in cited literature; no later resolution located.

**Last checked:** 2026-09-13

The March 2026 constructions produce a prescribed periodic invariant curve, not a foliation of the whole exterior. Rigidity theorems for outer-area and symplectic billiards concern different maps. Searches included “outer length billiards totally integrable ellipse 2026”, “fourth billiard rigidity”, and the Bialy–Tabachnikov title. No full rigidity theorem or nonelliptic example was located.
