# 018. The three-dimensional Lieb–Thirring constant

**Area:** Quantum mechanics and spectral inequalities

**Status:** 🔵 OPEN

**Last checked:** 2026-09-08

## Problem statement

For a real potential $V\in L^{5/2}(\mathbb R^3)$, define $H=-\Delta+V$ on $L^2(\mathbb R^3)$ by its quadratic form. Let $(E_j)$ be all its negative eigenvalues, with multiplicity, and $V_-(x)=\max\{-V(x),0\}$. Prove or disprove

$$
\sum_j|E_j|\le\frac{1}{15\pi^2}\int_{\mathbb R^3}V_-(x)^{5/2}\,dx.
$$

The proposed coefficient is the semiclassical phase-space constant $L^{\rm cl}_{1,3}$; it is already a necessary lower bound for any universal coefficient.

## Application

The sharp estimate controls total bound-state energy and is dual to a sharp fermionic kinetic-energy bound, central to the Thomas–Fermi approximation and stability of matter.

## Status review

**Literature check:** Open in cited literature; no later resolution located.

The sharp-constant review leaves this physically relevant case open. The August 2025 paper obtains the semiclassical constant for shifted Coulomb Hamiltonians, not arbitrary potentials. Recent disproofs of some other parameter versions of the original Lieb–Thirring conjecture, and complex-potential analogues, do not settle this particular real-potential inequality.
