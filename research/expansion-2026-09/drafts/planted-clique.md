# Polynomial-time detection below the planted-clique square-root threshold

**Area:** Statistical inference and average-case computational complexity

**Status:** Open in cited literature; no later resolution located as of 2026-09-17.

**Last checked:** 2026-09-17

## Problem statement

Fix $0<\varepsilon<1/2$ and set $k_n=\lfloor n^{1/2-\varepsilon}\rfloor$. Under the null distribution $P_{0,n}$, a labeled simple graph on $n$ vertices has each edge independently with probability $1/2$. Under $P_{1,n}$, first sample that graph, choose an independent uniformly random $k_n$-element vertex set, and add all missing edges inside that set.

The detection version of the planted-clique conjecture asserts that every randomized polynomial-time algorithm $A$, outputting zero or one from the observed graph, satisfies

$$
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left[
P_{0,n}\{A(G)=1\}+P_{1,n}\{A(G)=0\}
\right]\ge 1.
$$

The probabilities include the algorithm's internal randomness. The algorithm and $\varepsilon$ are fixed before taking the limit; polynomial time is measured in the adjacency-matrix input size. Thus no such algorithm achieves a nonvanishing advantage over guessing under equal prior probabilities. The conjecture permits advantages tending to zero and is a detection question: the algorithm need not identify the planted vertices. This fixed power-law formulation is contained explicitly in the sequence formulation of Brennan–Bresler–Huleihel and Huleihel–Mazumdar–Pal.

## Applied significance

This is a basic model of detecting a dense anomalous group in a noisy network. It also supplies a conjectural foundation for reductions explaining why statistically detectable signals may remain hard to find efficiently, including submatrix detection and sparse inference. Such consequences remain conditional on the hardness conjecture.

## Status review

The September 17, 2026 review searched planted/hidden-clique aliases, Jerrum and Kučera spellings, detection and square-root wording, proof/disproof terms, 2025–2026 developments, unrestricted results, corrections and version records. The exact risk conjecture is stated in the first two references. Independent work of Hirahara–Shimizu proves relations among variants without proving the hardness assertion. The April 2026 geometric-graph paper also distinguishes established lower bounds for particular algorithms from the unresolved general Erdős–Rényi barrier.

Known polynomial-time algorithms recover cliques at constant multiples of $\sqrt n$. Lower bounds for specific methods, including recent sum-of-squares results, do not cover all randomized polynomial-time algorithms. The geometric-graph theorem exploits dependent edges generated by spatial proximity, rather than the independent background in this statement. The 2025 result of Agrimonti–Bressan–D’Orsi for arbitrary backgrounds also has no useful displayed guarantee after substituting $k_n=n^{1/2-\varepsilon}$; the exact comparison is in the audit record.

Hirahara–Shimizu discuss a simple edge-counting advantage of order $k^2/n$, which tends to zero in this regime. That observation invalidates some stronger negligible-advantage claims, but does not refute the present nonvanishing-advantage formulation. Fixed-size, binomial-size and adversarial-size planting must be distinguished when quoting their results.
