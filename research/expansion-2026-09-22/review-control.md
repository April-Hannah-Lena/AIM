# Cross-review of control, transport and geometric PDE additions

Review date: 22 September 2026. A second agent read all 35 statements, bibliographies and status reviews independently of their drafting agent. This is an editorial and mathematical-formulation review, not specialist peer review or proof certification.

## Scope checked

- The wave and Schrödinger dynamic-boundary questions use different speed regimes and evolution equations; their observations, energies and exterior boundaries are explicit.
- The shallow-water question includes endpoint compatibility and the fixed normalization for its proposed time threshold.
- Smooth semigeostrophic continuation and bounded weak uniqueness concern different solution classes and conclusions.
- Polyhedral full transport, partial-transport free boundaries, injectivity-domain convexity and continuity under already convex injectivity domains are separate assertions.
- The planar Monge–Ampère question asks for a fixed weak-integrability endpoint, rather than the known unspecified higher integrability. A malformed LaTeX escape in its bibliography was corrected.
- The kinetic control question asks for exact arrival, and the KdV question concerns uncontrolled decay. Neither is replaced by the cited approximate-control or exact-controllability results.
- The viscoelastic question has a prescribed transport field and no target for stress. The obstacle question specifies an admissible endpoint-control class and the exact stationary target.
- The thermal shape problems have different geometric constraints and boundary laws; known maximization or unconstrained-insulation results do not answer them.
- The two fractional logarithmic-convexity questions distinguish nonsymmetric spatial drift from coupled time orders.
- The mean-field-game questions separate locally uniform value convergence in the presence of vacuum from a quantitative kinetic density convergence rate.
- The minimal-surface questions distinguish inner stationarity, polynomial growth, exact polynomial solutions, and anisotropic constitutive laws. A dimension change alone is not being counted as a separate problem.
- The complex Monge–Ampère question omits the real-convexity assumption of the September 2026 result.
- The analytic-control selection and polynomial reachable-ball questions are distinct from existing entry 143 on finite-jet determination.
- The two weak-diffusion control questions distinguish a sharp one-dimensional time from a multidimensional non-gradient Neumann-flow theorem.
- The irreversible catalytic reaction has strictly positive initial data. This removes the boundary-equilibrium counterexample allowed by the source’s broader printed wording. It is not a complex-balanced network.
- The two dispersion-managed equations ask about norm inflation in three dimensions and uniqueness of a one-dimensional zero-average variational ground state.

The reviewer reopened the Nguyen–Tang July 2026 article, Jafarpour’s primary full text, Kowalski’s Gabitov–Turitsyn paper, and the KIT dispersion-management project page. The latter is an archived 2015–2019 program description, not a new 2026 open-status assertion; its role was clarified. A symmetry clarification for the zero-average variational problem was referred to the drafting agent.

No duplicate of an active catalogue statement was identified in this group. The corrections were incorporated and the catalogue-wide checks recorded in the expansion audit passed.

## Resolution of review comments

The malformed LaTeX was corrected. The KIT source is labelled as archived, and the optical uniqueness question now includes reflection and conjugate time reversal in the symmetry group. All 35 pages passed the mathematical-notation parse check.
