# Two-fold replacement: interface correction and an exact overlap criterion

Astra research, 2026-09-08. Scope: the third positive Neumann eigenvalue in dimension two, with mu_0=0. This is a proved conditional variational theorem, NOT a proof of the required center/overlap existence statement or of Pólya for arbitrary domains.

## 1. A binary partition tree is not the interface graph

The three planar cells
C_1={x<0}, C_2={x>0,y>0}, C_3={x>0,y<0}
are produced by a binary split followed by a split of one child. Yet every pair shares an open interface segment: their geometric adjacency graph is a triangle. The same example works inside a disk or a square. Thus Sol 3's assertion that a hierarchical binary partition automatically has tree adjacency is false. Its proved obstruction for Voronoi fields is unaffected; the asserted escape from it was not justified.

There is a more precise affine-fold obstruction. Write H={x:n.x=c}, with unit normal n, and define the continuous fold into H^+ by
f_H(x)=x+2(c-n.x)_+ n.
It fixes H^+ and reflects H^-.

Suppose the first fold maps both original halfspaces onto H_1^+, and we apply a second fold f_K only on the branch originally lying in H_1^+, leaving the other branch unchanged. On the original interface H_1 the traces become f_K(x) and x. They agree if and only if H_1 is contained in K^+, the fixed halfspace of f_K. For complete affine hyperplanes this requires K parallel to H_1 (possibly coincident), with the appropriate orientation. If K is transverse, a relatively open part of H_1 has unequal traces, so this piecewise affine map is not H^1 locally across that part.

This statement concerns this precise affine-map construction. On a fixed Omega the bad portion might lie outside Omega; and special radial fields could have extra degeneracies. Neither possibility has been excluded. The conclusion is not a universal prohibition of hierarchical methods.

For comparison, after folding x to |x|, fold only the positive original branch at x=a>0 back into x<=a. The resulting first coordinate is -x for x<0, x for 0<x<a, and 2a-x for x>a. This is a continuous three-piece parallel construction. It has fewer geometric parameters and gives no center theorem by itself.

## 2. The global composition is genuinely continuous

Instead use
phi=f_{H_2} o f_{H_1}
on ALL of R^2. Each fold is 1-Lipschitz and piecewise an affine isometry. Hence phi is 1-Lipschitz and admits a partition, up to finitely many lines, into at most four measurable pieces P_s on each of which phi_s is an affine isometry.

For a bounded Lipschitz domain Omega of area V>0 define the pushforward density
rho(y)=sum_s 1_{phi_s(Omega intersect P_s)}(y).
Then, almost everywhere,
0<=rho<=4, integral rho=V,
and for every nonnegative measurable h,
integral_Omega h(phi(x)) dx = integral_R2 rho(y)h(y)dy.
This is a finite sum of ordinary changes of variables; no uncontrolled limit or smooth inverse is required.

There can really be four sheets: perpendicular folds through the origin give phi(x,y)=(|x|,|y|). On a centered disk the density is 4 on its first quadrant. Consequently one must not replace the upper bound 4 by 3 merely because the intended spectral index is 3.

Put
e=integral (rho-3)_+ = |{rho=4}|, theta=e/V.
Here e is IMAGE area, not the total area of the four preimage sheets. Since 4e<=V, 0<=theta<=1/4. Split rho=rho_0+eta, where rho_0=min(rho,3) has mass V-e, 0<=rho_0<=3, and eta has mass e.

## 3. A rational radial profile

Let R^2=(V-e)/(3*pi), and define
g(s)=s-s^3/3 for 0<=s<=1, and g(s)=2/3 for s>=1.
For A in R^2 set
W_A(y)=g(|y-A|/R)(y-A)/|y-A|, W_A(A)=0.
This field is globally Lipschitz. Its squared norm is increasing with radius. With t=(r/R)^2, its squared derivative norm is

H_R(r)=R^(-2)[2-(8/3)t+(10/9)t^2] for r<=R,
H_R(r)=4/(9r^2) for r>=R.

Indeed |DW|_F^2=G'(r)^2+G(r)^2/r^2. The inside derivative with respect to t is -8/3+(20/9)t<=-4/9; the inside and outside values agree at R. Thus H_R is decreasing everywhere, nonnegative, and bounded by 2/R^2. Also g'=1-s^2>=0 on [0,1], with matching derivative zero at the join.

Exact radial integrals over B_R(A) are
integral |W_A|^2 = (11*pi*R^2)/36,
integral |DW_A|_F^2 = 28*pi/27.
No Bessel root, numerical eigenvalue, or external stability theorem enters these formulas.

## 4. Conditional third-mode theorem, with full proof

Let u_0,u_1,u_2 be any real L^2-orthonormal Neumann eigenfunctions on Omega corresponding to the first three eigenvalues with multiplicity. In particular u_0=V^(-1/2). Suppose the two folds and A satisfy all six scalar equations
integral_Omega W_A(phi(x)) u_l(x) dx = 0 in R^2, l=0,1,2.

Set F=W_A o phi. Lipschitz continuity gives F in H^1(Omega;R^2). On each isometry piece the Sobolev chain rule gives
|F|^2=|W_A(phi)|^2, |DF|_F^2=|DW_A(phi)|_F^2.
The exceptional lines have measure zero. No unaccounted interface energy is present.

Each component is orthogonal to u_0,u_1,u_2, including when mu_1 or mu_2 is multiple. Componentwise min--max and summation imply
mu_3 integral |F|^2 <= integral |DF|_F^2.

For completeness the density rearrangements need only the following elementary comparison. If 0<=q<=3 and integral q=3|B_R(A)|, then for radial increasing a(r),
integral q a >= 3 integral_B_R a.
Subtract a(R) from the integrand: outside the ball both factors q and a-a(R) are nonnegative; inside both q-3 and a-a(R) are nonpositive. For a decreasing h the same argument reverses the inequality. This proves both comparisons without assuming that q is a characteristic function or that Omega is star-shaped.

Apply them to rho_0 and the two radial functions above. The denominator is at least
D_0=3*(11*pi*R^2/36)=11(V-e)/36>0.
The numerator is at most
3*(28*pi/27)+(2/R^2)e.
Discarding the nonnegative eta contribution only in the denominator yields the rigorous estimate
mu_3(Omega)*V <= 3*pi/(1-theta) *
 [112/33 + (72/11)*theta/(1-theta)].                         (1)

In particular (1) implies mu_3 V<=12*pi whenever
5-92*theta+33*theta^2>=0,
or equivalently, for the admissible range 0<=theta<=1/4,
theta <= theta_*=(46-sqrt(1951))/33.

A convenient rational sufficient condition is e<=V/20. The right-hand side of (1) increases with theta, and at theta=1/20 it equals
(46880/3971)*pi < 12*pi,
with exact margin (772/3971)*pi. Thus all six moments plus this overlap budget prove a strict planar third-mode Pólya bound.

This theorem is slightly more general than two folds: any globally Lipschitz piecewise isometry with at most four sheets obeys the same conclusion when its six moments and overlap budget hold.

## 4b. Sharper exact rearrangement: a larger overlap budget

The coarse estimate above discarded information about eta. In fact 0<=eta<=1 and its mass is e. Apply the same radial comparison with capacity 1 on the disk of radius r_e=sqrt(e/pi). Set
q=r_e^2/R^2=3*theta/(1-theta), so 0<=q<=1.
The numerator and denominator now satisfy

N <= pi*[28/9+2q-(4/3)q^2+(10/27)q^3],
D >= pi*R^2*[11/12+q^2/2-(2/9)q^3+q^4/36].

Thus, writing the bracketed polynomials as n(q), d(q),
mu_3 V/pi <= (3+q)*n(q)/d(q).                            (2)
All rearrangements can be saturated simultaneously at the abstract density
rho=3*1_{B_R(A)}+1_{B_{r_e}(A)}.
This proves sharpness of the density comparison for this fixed profile and these density constraints, NOT spectral sharpness or geometric realizability with the required moments.

The right-hand side of (2) is at most 12 precisely when
P(q)=q^4+66q^3-216q^2+246q-45 <= 0.
Indeed 27*((3+q)*n(q)-12*d(q))=P(q).
On [0,1],
P'(q)=16+24(1-q)+(206+4q)(1-q)^2 >=16.
Since P(0)<0<P(1), there is a unique q_* in (0,1); the exact allowable budget is theta<=q_*/(3+q_*).

In particular theta<=1/15 gives q<=3/14 and
P(3/14)=-59643/38416<0.
So the SAME SIX MOMENTS together with e<=V/15 suffice for the planar third-mode Pólya inequality. This strictly improves the elementary 1/20 budget above. Exact comparisons P(3/14)<0<P(3/13) locate the best budget from this polynomial profile between 1/15 and 1/14, without any floating-point root calculation.

## 5. The mean equations can be solved uniquely, leaving four moments

For ANY fixed two folds, not just symmetric examples, the two u_0 equations have a unique solution A. Define
Psi_R(r)=integral_0^r G_R(s)ds
and J(A)=integral_Omega Psi_R(|phi(x)-A|)dx.
The function Psi_R is convex, strictly increasing for positive r, continuously differentiable, and grows linearly at infinity with slope 2/3. Thus J is differentiable and coercive, with gradient
grad J(A)=-integral_Omega W_A(phi(x))dx.

J is strictly convex: for distinct A,B the strict triangle inequality holds for every y outside the line through A,B. Combining it with strict increase and convexity of Psi_R gives strict convexity of its integral, because the pushforward rho has positive two-dimensional mass and assigns zero mass to that line. Hence J has exactly one minimizer, denoted A(H_1,H_2), and it cancels the mean. A supporting-line argument also places A in the interior of the convex hull of the pushforward support: otherwise every projected W_A has one sign and a positive-area set has strictly positive sign.

This center depends continuously on the two oriented lines, parametrized by (n_i,c_i) in (S^1 times R)^2. Here is the needed dependence of the radius, which must not be omitted. The four branch sets are intersections of Omega with finitely many moving halfspaces. At any fixed parameter their indicator functions converge almost everywhere off finitely many lines and hence in L^1. Their affine isometries vary continuously, and translations and rotations act continuously in L^1. Therefore rho varies in L^1, e=integral(rho-3)_+ varies continuously, and so does R. Locally all image supports and R lie in bounded sets, and R is bounded away from zero since e<=V/4. The corresponding functions J converge uniformly on compact sets; their minimizers stay locally bounded by the convex-hull bound. Uniqueness then implies continuity of A.

Consequently the remaining map
M(H_1,H_2)=(integral F u_1, integral F u_2) in R^4
is continuous, by bounded convergence (|F|<=2/3 and u_l in L^1(Omega)). The mean condition is now automatic, not an additional conjecture. The precise sufficient existence statement is:

There is a zero of this FOUR-dimensional continuous map on the subset e/V<=1/15 (or the larger quartic-threshold subset) of (S^1 times R)^2.

Neither the admissible set's compactness nor a nonzero degree is proved. Continuity and a matching dimension alone are insufficient.

## 6. Why the conditions are still substantial

The four remaining moments and the overlap constraint must hold at the SAME pair of folds. The unique mean center in Section 5 does not enforce these signed eigenfunction moments. No compact parameter space, boundary behavior, nonzero degree, or continuation theorem has been proved.

On a centered disk the perpendicular diameter folds have e=V/4, well outside the budget. They make F even in both input coordinates, so the u_1,u_2 moments vanish for every A, using the standard angular first eigenspace. The mean can also be canceled: minimize over A the coercive convex function integral_Omega Psi(|phi(x)-A|) dx, where Psi'=G. Its differentiability and coercivity give a minimizer with integral F=0. Thus a familiar exact moment solution need not have low overlap. This is NOT a proof that all disk moment solutions violate the budget.

The new bottleneck is precise: construct (or refute the universal existence of) a zero of the reduced four-moment map with e/V<=1/15, or more generally the quartic threshold of Section 4b. Failing this, use actual spatial information to improve upon the abstract density bound. Counting parameters or reporting sampled moment residuals does not settle existence.
