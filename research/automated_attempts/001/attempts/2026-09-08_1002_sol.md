# Sol attempt 2 — the global third-mode stability constant cannot close the planar gap

- UTC run: 2026-09-08 10:02
- Current problem: 001, Pólya's eigenvalue inequalities
- Starting state revision: 2
- Target: quantify the lower-side stability route prescribed by Astra, specialized to area \(\pi\), \(d=2\), \(k=3\), and determine whether de Villeroché's Theorem 1.1 can cover the whole interval \(2j_{0,1}^2\le\lambda_2\le12\).

## 1. Normalization and the published estimate

Let \(D\) be the unit disk and let \(\Theta\) be the union of two disks of area \(\pi/2\). Set
\[
 a=j_{0,1}^2=\lambda_1(D),\qquad b=j_{1,1}^2=\lambda_2(D)=\lambda_3(D),
\]
so that
\[
 A:=\lambda_2(\Theta)=2a,\qquad B:=\lambda_3(\Theta)=2b.
\]

Theorem 1.1 of de Villeroché, [arXiv:2506.05870v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05870v3), uses a single dimension-dependent constant \(C_2\), independent of \(k\), and in dimension two states
\[
 |\lambda_k(\Omega)-\lambda_k(\Theta)|
 \le C_2 k^4\lambda_2(\Omega)^{17/18}
 (\lambda_2(\Omega)-A)^{1/18}. \tag{1}
\]
For a would-be Pólya counterexample of area \(\pi\) with \(\lambda_3(\Omega)\le12\), (1) at \(k=3\) implies
\[
 \lambda_2(\Omega)-A\ge
 \eta(C_2):=
 \left(\frac{B-12}{81C_2\,12^{17/18}}\right)^{18}. \tag{2}
\]
The previous review left open whether \(\eta(C_2)\) could reach the full available width \(12-A\).

## 2. Exact disk trial bounds

The radial trial family
\[
 f_c(r)=(1-r^2)(1+cr^2)
\]
has, after \(t=r^2\), exact Rayleigh quotient
\[
 \mathcal R(f_c)=20\frac{c^2+2c+3}{c^2+5c+10}.
\]
At \(c=-2/5\),
\[
 a<\mathcal R(f_{-2/5})=\frac{295}{51}. \tag{3}
\]
The inequality is strict because the polynomial is not a disk eigenfunction. The exact Rayleigh sum
\[
 \sum_{n\ge1}j_{0,n}^{-4}=\frac1{32}
\]
gives
\[
 a>4\sqrt2. \tag{4}
\]
For the angular trial \(g(r,\theta)=r(1-r^2)\cos\theta\), direct integration gives \(\mathcal R(g)=16\), hence
\[
 b<16. \tag{5}
\]
Finally \(b>2a\) by the strict Hong--Krahn--Szegő inequality applied to the connected disk. In particular all fractional powers below have positive arguments.

Equations (3)--(4) give the exact width estimates
\[
 12-A=12-2a>12-\frac{590}{51}=\frac{22}{51}>\frac14. \tag{6}
\]

## 3. A necessary lower bound on every valid global constant

Apply (1) not at \(k=3\), but at \(k=1\) and \(\Omega=D\). Since \(\lambda_1(\Theta)=2a\), it gives
\[
 a\le C_2 b^{17/18}(b-2a)^{1/18}.
\]
Both \(b\) and \(b-2a\) are strictly below \(16\), so
\[
 C_2\ge\frac{a}{b^{17/18}(b-2a)^{1/18}}
 >\frac{a}{16}>\frac{\sqrt2}{4}. \tag{7}
\]
This lower bound applies to the best possible constant in the stated all-\(k\) theorem; it is not an artifact of failing to track constants through the proof.

## 4. The exclusion strip is necessarily much too short

By (5), \(B-12=2b-12<20\). Also \(12^{17/18}>2\). Substituting these inequalities and (7) into (2) yields
\[
 \eta(C_2)
 <\left(\frac{20}{81(\sqrt2/4)\,2}\right)^{18}
 =\left(\frac{40}{81\sqrt2}\right)^{18}
 <2^{-18}. \tag{8}
\]
The last comparison is equivalent to \(80^2<2\cdot81^2\). Combining (6) and (8),
\[
 \eta(C_2)<2^{-18}<\frac14<12-A. \tag{9}
\]

Therefore **no constant valid in the published all-index Theorem 1.1 can make its direct exclusion strip cover the planar \(k=3\) interval**. This remains true even if every hidden constant in the proof is optimized. The obstruction comes from sharing one \(C_2\) across all indices: the unit disk at \(k=1\) already forces it to be too large.

This is stronger than the observation that the published proof is non-effective. That proof also reuses \(C_d\) from line to line and contains an unspecified large cutting length and a tangent-ball \(O(\delta^{d+1})\) step, so it does not presently expose a numerical value. But reconstructing those constants cannot overcome (9).

## 5. What remains viable

The result does **not** rule out a specialized one-sided \(k=3\) stability estimate. If
\[
 B-\lambda_3(\Omega)
 \le 81C_{2,3}\lambda_2(\Omega)^{17/18}
 (\lambda_2(\Omega)-A)^{1/18}
\]
is proved only on the restricted class \(A\le\lambda_2\le12\), then it would close the interval provided
\[
 C_{2,3}\le C_{\rm req}:=
 \frac{B-12}{81\,12^{17/18}(12-A)^{1/18}}. \tag{10}
\]
The disk \(k=1\) obstruction no longer applies to such a coefficient. A more natural new target may be the one-sided square-root estimate
\[
 B-\lambda_3(\Omega)\le K\sqrt{\lambda_2(\Omega)-A}, \tag{11}
\]
which would suffice if \(K\le(B-12)/\sqrt{12-A}\). Neither (10) nor (11) is proved here.

## Rigor status

### Established

1. Exact polynomial bounds (3), (5), the Rayleigh-sum bound (4), and width bound (6).
2. Every constant in de Villeroché's stated all-\(k\) dimension-two theorem satisfies \(C_2>\sqrt2/4\).
3. Consequently its direct \(k=3\) exclusion width is always less than \(2^{-18}\), whereas full coverage requires more than \(22/51\).
4. Extracting or optimizing the theorem's shared global constant is therefore an exhausted route for closing planar \(k=3\).

### External dependency
