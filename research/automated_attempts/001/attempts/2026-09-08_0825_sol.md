## Target of this run

Establish rigorous arbitrary-domain cases from the recorded ball theorem and sharp fixed-volume shape inequalities; extract a reusable criterion for further Dirichlet indices; and test whether the evident extension through unions of equal balls can continue to the first open index.

Put

```math
P_k(V):=4\pi^2\left(\frac{k}{\omega_dV}\right)^{2/d},
\qquad \nu=\frac d2-1,
```

and let $`B_v`$ denote a ball of volume $`v`$. The ball case recorded in the source problem gives, in particular,

```math
\mu_1(B_v)\le P_1(v)\le \lambda_1(B_v).
```

## 1. Complete two-sided result for $`k=1,2`$

The four sharp shape inequalities needed are

```math
\begin{aligned}
 \lambda_1(\Omega)&\ge \lambda_1(B_V),
 &\lambda_2(\Omega)&\ge \lambda_1(B_{V/2}),\\
 \mu_1(\Omega)&\le \mu_1(B_V),
 &\mu_2(\Omega)&\le \mu_1(B_{V/2}),
\end{aligned}
\qquad V=|\Omega|.
```

The first row is Faber--Krahn and Hong--Krahn--Szegő. The first inequality in the second row is Szegő--Weinberger. The last is Theorem 1 of Bucur--Henrot, *Maximization of the second non-trivial Neumann eigenvalue*, Acta Math. 222 (2019), arXiv:1801.07435. Their "regular set" hypothesis is compactness of $`H^1(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^2(\Omega)`$, explicitly including bounded Lipschitz sets, and their indexing is $`0=\mu_0\le\mu_1\le\mu_2\le\cdots`$, exactly as here.

Since $`P_1(V/2)=P_2(V)`$, the ball inequalities and the four shape inequalities give

```math
\mu_k(\Omega)\le P_k(V)\le\lambda_k(\Omega),\qquad k=1,2,
```

for every bounded connected Lipschitz $`\Omega\subset\mathbb R^d`$, $`d\ge2`$. This is a complete verification of both sides at the first two indices, not a numerical result.

## 2. A dimension-dependent Dirichlet band

Let $`j_{\nu,1}`$ be the first positive zero of $`J_\nu`$, and define

```math
\beta_d:=\frac{j_{\nu,1}^{\,d}\omega_d^2}{(2\pi)^d}.
```

### Nodal-count lemma

If an eigenfunction for $`\lambda_k(\Omega)`$ has $`r`$ nodal domains, then

```math
\lambda_k(\Omega)\ge
j_{\nu,1}^2\left(\frac{r\omega_d}{V}\right)^{2/d}.
\tag{1}
```

Consequently the Dirichlet Pólya bound at index $`k`$ holds whenever

```math
k\le r\beta_d. \tag{2}
```

Proof: on every nodal domain $`D_i`$, the sign-definite restriction is a ground state and $`\lambda_1(D_i)=\lambda_k(\Omega)`$. Faber--Krahn gives

```math
|D_i|\ge \omega_d j_{\nu,1}^{\,d}\lambda_k^{-d/2}.
```

Sum over the $`r`$ disjoint nodal domains to obtain (1). Raising the comparison of (1) with $`P_k(V)`$ to the power $`d/2`$ gives (2).

Because $`\Omega`$ is connected, $`\lambda_1`$ is simple and every eigenfunction above the ground state changes sign. Thus $`r\ge2`$ for every $`k\ge2`$. It follows that

```math
P_k(V)\le\lambda_k(\Omega)
\quad\text{for every integer }2\le k\le\lfloor2\beta_d\rfloor.
\tag{3}
```

The recorded ball result at $`k=1`$ implies $`\beta_d\ge1`$, so the band always includes $`k=2`$.

Two exact low-dimensional consequences are:

- In $`d=2`$, $`2\beta_2=j_{0,1}^2/2\in(2,3)`$, so this mechanism gives exactly $`k\le2`$.
- In $`d=3`$, $`j_{1/2,1}=\pi`$, $`\omega_3=4\pi/3`$, and $`2\beta_3=4\pi^2/9\in(4,5)`$. Hence the Dirichlet Pólya inequality holds for every $`k\le4`$ on every bounded connected Lipschitz domain in $`\mathbb R^3`$.

More generally, (2) makes nodal information directly usable: any eigenvalue admitting an eigenfunction with at least $`\lceil k/\beta_d\rceil`$ nodal domains satisfies the conjectured Dirichlet bound.

## 3. Rigorous failure of the equal-ball extension at planar $`k=3`$

A tempting continuation would assert

```math
\lambda_k(\Omega)\ge\lambda_1(B_{V/k}),
\tag{4}
```

generalizing Hong--Krahn--Szegő. This is already false for $`k=3`$ in the plane.

Let $`D`$ be the unit disk and use polar coordinates. In $`H_0^1(D)`$, consider

```math
f_0=1-r^2,\qquad
f_1=r(1-r^2)\cos\theta,\qquad
f_2=r(1-r^2)\sin\theta.
```

The three functions are mutually orthogonal both in $`L^2`$ and for the Dirichlet energy. Direct integration gives

```math
\mathcal R(f_0)=6,\qquad
\mathcal R(f_1)=\mathcal R(f_2)=16.
```

Therefore the min--max principle yields $`\lambda_3(D)\le16`$.

A ball of area $`\pi/3`$ has first eigenvalue $`3j_{0,1}^2`$. The classical Rayleigh sum for the positive zeros $`j_{0,n}`$ of $`J_0`$,

```math
\sum_{n\ge1}j_{0,n}^{-4}=\frac1{32},
```

implies $`j_{0,1}^4>32`$, hence

```math
3j_{0,1}^2>12\sqrt2>16\ge\lambda_3(D).
```

Thus (4) fails strictly without using decimal approximations. The obstruction is not to Pólya itself: (4) asks for a stronger constant. It shows that the successful $`k=2`$ transfer cannot be iterated by simply replacing two equal balls by $`k`$ equal balls.

For completeness, the planar threshold above is exact for this mechanism: the trial function $`f_0`$ gives $`j_{0,1}^2<6`$ (strictly, since $`f_0`$ is not an eigenfunction), while the same Rayleigh sum gives $`j_{0,1}^2>4\sqrt2>4`$. Hence $`j_{0,1}^2/2\in(2,3)`$.

## What is rigorously established

1. The full Dirichlet--Neumann Pólya chain for arbitrary bounded connected Lipschitz domains at $`k=1,2`$, in every $`d\ge2`$.
2. The nodal-count criterion (1)--(2).
3. The universal Dirichlet band (3), including $`k\le4`$ in dimension three.
4. A fully analytic counterexample to the proposed stronger inequality (4) at $`(d,k)=(2,3)`$.

These are programme results assembled from proved shape theorems plus the derivations above; no claim of novelty in the literature is made.

## What is conjectural or computational

No computational claim is used. A possible next lead is that the third spectral degree of freedom should rule out the near-two-ball geometry that makes the $`\lambda_2`$ bound sharp, but no quantitative version has been proved here.

## What failed

The generalized "$`k`$ equal balls minimize $`\lambda_k`$" route fails at the first new planar index. Courant's theorem also cannot substitute $`r=k`$: it supplies the opposite inequality $`r\le k`$.

## Precise remaining gap

The first index for which the full two-sided chain remains untreated is $`k=3`$. The Neumann side lacks an analogue of the Bucur--Henrot two-center theorem at $`\mu_3`$. On the Dirichlet side, (3) settles $`k=3`$ in every dimension for which $`3\le2\beta_d`$, including all of $`d=3`$; the first concrete gap is $`d=2,k=3`$, where separate two-nodal-domain Faber--Krahn estimates stop at

```math
\lambda_3(\Omega)|\Omega|\ge2\pi j_{0,1}^2<12\pi.
```
