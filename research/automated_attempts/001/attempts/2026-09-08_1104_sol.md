## 1. Exact variational reduction

Let `u_0=|Omega|^{-1/2}` and choose orthonormal Neumann eigenfunctions `u_1,u_2` for the first two positive spectral slots (counting multiplicity). If a vector field `F=(f_1,...,f_d)` belongs to `H^1(Omega;R^d)` and
\[
 \int_\Omega F u_\ell\,dx=0\in\mathbb R^d,
 \qquad \ell=0,1,2,                                      \tag{1}
\]
then every component lies in the spectral complement of `u_0,u_1,u_2`. Hence
\[
 \mu_3(\Omega)\int_\Omega |F|^2
 \le \int_\Omega |DF|_{\mathrm F}^2.                    \tag{2}
\]
Thus center selection for `mu_3` is exactly the `3d`-equation moment problem (1). It is logically separate from constructing an admissible field with the sharp radial quotient.

Let `r_3=(|Omega|/(3\omega_d))^{1/d}`. For the first nonzero Neumann ball profile, write
\[
 W_A(x)=G(|x-A|)\frac{x-A}{|x-A|},
\]
where `G` is the increasing radial profile on `[0,r_3]`, continued constantly afterwards. On a region carrying `QW_A`, with `Q` orthogonal,
\[
 |QW_A|^2=G(|x-A|)^2,
 \qquad |D(QW_A)|_{\mathrm F}^2
 =G'(|x-A|)^2+(d-1)\frac{G(|x-A|)^2}{|x-A|^2}.           \tag{3}
\]
Consequently, after (1), the remaining analytic target is a three-cell mass-displacement estimate making the quotient in (2) at most `mu_1(B_{r_3})`. This would imply the desired Pólya bound because the union of three equal balls has `mu_3=mu_1(B_{r_3})` and the ball case is known.

## 2. Canonical three-center fold

Take three distinct noncollinear sites `A_1,A_2,A_3` and their Voronoi cells `V_i`. For `i!=j`, let
\[
 R_{ij}=I-2n_{ij}\otimes n_{ij},\qquad
 n_{ij}=\frac{A_j-A_i}{|A_j-A_i|},                       \tag{4}
\]
the linear reflection parallel to the perpendicular bisector of `A_iA_j`. The direct analogue of the two-center construction is
\[
 F(x)=Q_iW_{A_i}(x),\qquad x\in V_i,                     \tag{5}
\]
with constant orthogonal frames `Q_i`. Orthogonality is precisely what preserves the radial identities (3). Allowing arbitrary invertible constant frames does not repair the gluing problem below.

### Proposition (odd-cycle holonomy obstruction)

For `d>=2` and three noncollinear sites, there are no invertible matrices `Q_1,Q_2,Q_3` for which (5) has matching traces on all three pairwise Voronoi faces. In particular, the canonical three-center reflected field is not in `H^1_loc(R^d;R^d)` for any choice of orthogonal frames.

### Proof

On the bisector `H_{ij}`, equal distances and (4) give
\[
 x-A_j=R_{ij}(x-A_i),\qquad W_{A_j}(x)=R_{ij}W_{A_i}(x). \tag{6}
\]
For three noncollinear sites every pairwise Voronoi face has nonempty relative interior: on `H_{ij}`, the difference `|x-A_k|^2-|x-A_i|^2` is a nonconstant affine function, so it is positive on a relatively open half of the bisector. The trace vectors `W_{A_i}(x)` on such an open face span `R^d`: after division by the positive scalar `G(|x-A_i|)/|x-A_i|`, they contain a relatively open subset of the affine hyperplane `(x-A_i)\cdot n_{ij}=|A_j-A_i|/2`, whose linear span is all of `R^d`.

Piecewise Lipschitz fields are in `H^1_loc` only if their two traces agree almost everywhere on each codimension-one interface; otherwise the distributional derivative contains a nonzero surface measure. Equations (5)--(6), plus the spanning property, therefore force
\[
 Q_i=Q_jR_{ij}                                           \tag{7}
\]
on each face. Traversing the cycle `1 -> 2 -> 3 -> 1` yields
\[
 R_{12}R_{23}R_{31}=I.                                  \tag{8}
\]
But every `R_{ij}` is a hyperplane reflection, so
\[
 \det(R_{12}R_{23}R_{31})=(-1)^3=-1,
\]
contradicting (8). This proves the proposition. `square`

The same argument applies to any odd cycle in a Voronoi adjacency graph whose face traces span `R^d`. It is a parity obstruction, not a delicate failure at a triple junction: inconsistent pairwise traces occur on a positive `(d-1)`-dimensional portion of at least one face.

## 3. Stress tests and scope

- The contradiction uses noncollinearity. For three collinear sites the Voronoi adjacency graph is a path, and constant frames can be propagated along its two edges. The space of ordered collinear triples has dimension `2d+1`, hence codimension `d-1` inside the generic `3d`-parameter space; this dimension count is not a proof that the `3d` moment equations (1) have no solution there.
- A hierarchical binary partition also has a tree adjacency graph and avoids odd-cycle holonomy. It is the clean surviving variant: fold across one mediator, then fold one child region across a second mediator. Whether its three pieces support the sharp equal-third mass displacement, and whether its parameters solve (1), are open.
- Position-dependent frames can interpolate the holonomy, but `D(Q(x)W_A)` acquires connection-energy terms. No estimate shows those terms fit beneath the sharp ball quotient.
- Singular constant frames are not excluded by the determinant proof, but they destroy the nonsingular radial frame used for the isotropic sum (3). No claim is made that every singular or scalar construction fails.
- For a fixed `Omega`, only faces meeting `Omega` in positive `(d-1)`-dimensional measure impose trace conditions. The proposition blocks a universal globally `H^1` field, and blocks a chosen triple whenever all three faces are active inside `Omega`; it does not exclude triples whose adjacency graph after restriction to `Omega` is a tree.
- This obstruction is to the natural three-center proof method, not to the Neumann Pólya inequality and not to every possible three-center test family.

## What failed

The most symmetric direct extension of the Bucur--Henrot two-center field to three noncollinear Voronoi centers cannot be made globally `H^1` by constant frames. Consequently a degree argument over generic triples cannot use this universal ansatz without first restricting to tree-like active adjacencies or changing the gluing mechanism, irrespective of the moment map.
