## 1. Counting implies every positive-order sharp Riesz inequality

For `gamma>0`, layer-cake gives, with endpoint values irrelevant to the integral,
\[
 R_{\gamma}^D(E):=\sum_j(E-\lambda_j)_+^\gamma
 =\gamma\int_0^E(E-t)^{\gamma-1}N_D^{\le}(t)\,dt,       \tag{1}
\]
and identically
\[
 R_{\gamma}^N(E)
 =\gamma\int_0^E(E-t)^{\gamma-1}N_N^{<}(t)\,dt.        \tag{2}
\]
Consequently the corresponding Pólya counting inequality implies, side by side,
\[
 R_\gamma^D(E)\le L_{\gamma,d}^{\rm cl}|\Omega|E^{\gamma+d/2},
 \qquad
 R_\gamma^N(E)\ge L_{\gamma,d}^{\rm cl}|\Omega|E^{\gamma+d/2}, \tag{3}
\]
where
\[
 L_{\gamma,d}^{\rm cl}
 =\frac{\Gamma(\gamma+1)}{(4\pi)^{d/2}\Gamma(\gamma+d/2+1)}. \tag{4}
\]
Indeed, (1)--(2) reduce (3) to the beta integral
\[
 \gamma C_d\int_0^E(E-t)^{\gamma-1}t^{d/2}\,dt
 =L_{\gamma,d}^{\rm cl}E^{\gamma+d/2}.                  \tag{5}
\]

This elementary implication is one-way: a fixed positive-order Riesz inequality need not recover counting, consistently with the recorded scalar countermodels.

## 2. Cartesian-product closure theorem

### Theorem

Let `Omega_1 subset R^a` and `Omega_2 subset R^b` be bounded connected Lipschitz domains, with `a,b>=1`. If the Dirichlet Pólya inequality holds at every index for each factor, then it holds at every index for `Omega_1 times Omega_2`. The same assertion holds independently for the Neumann Pólya inequality. Therefore, if both sides hold for both factors, the full chain holds for their product.

### Dirichlet proof

Tensorization of the Dirichlet quadratic forms gives the product spectrum
\[
 \{\lambda_j^{(1)}+\lambda_k^{(2)}:j,k\ge1\}
\]
with multiplicity. Thus, for `E>=0`,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 N_{D,1\times2}^{\le}(E)
 &=\sum_jN_{D,2}^{\le}(E-\lambda_j^{(1)})\\
 &\le C_b|\Omega_2|\sum_j(E-\lambda_j^{(1)})_+^{b/2}\\
 &=C_b|\Omega_2|R_{b/2}^{D,1}(E).                       \tag{6}
\end{aligned}
\]
Apply (3) to factor 1 with `gamma=b/2`. The constant simplifies exactly:
\[
 C_bL_{b/2,a}^{\rm cl}
 =\frac{(4\pi)^{-(a+b)/2}}{\Gamma((a+b)/2+1)}
 =C_{a+b}.                                               \tag{7}
\]
Since product volume is `|Omega_1||Omega_2|`, (6)--(7) give the desired counting inequality.

### Neumann proof

The product Neumann spectrum is `mu_j^(1)+mu_k^(2)`, now with `j,k>=0`. The strict count satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
 N_{N,1\times2}^{<}(E)
 &=\sum_jN_{N,2}^{<}(E-\mu_j^{(1)})\\
 &\ge C_b|\Omega_2|\sum_j(E-\mu_j^{(1)})_+^{b/2}\\
 &=C_b|\Omega_2|R_{b/2}^{N,1}(E).                       \tag{8}
\end{aligned}
\]
Equations (2)--(5) for factor 1 and the same constant identity (7) yield
`N_{N,1 times 2}^<(E)>=C_{a+b}|Omega_1||Omega_2|E^{(a+b)/2}`. The zero modes are counted correctly: for `E>0`, (2) includes the contribution `E^gamma` of `mu_0=0`. At `E=0` both sides vanish.

No boundary smoothness beyond the stated Lipschitz hypothesis is used: complete form eigenbases tensorize, and Cartesian products of bounded Lipschitz domains are bounded Lipschitz domains (corners are allowed).

## 3. New actual-domain consequences

1. The one-dimensional interval satisfies both counting inequalities exactly:
   \[
   N_D^{\le}(E)=\left\lfloor\frac{L\sqrt E}{\pi}\right\rfloor,
   \qquad
   N_N^{<}(E)=\left\lceil\frac{L\sqrt E}{\pi}\right\rceil.
   \]
   Hence adjoining any finite rectangular factor preserves every already-proved Pólya side.
2. Since Euclidean balls satisfy both Pólya inequalities in the recorded source baseline, every finite Cartesian product of Euclidean balls and intervals satisfies the full two-sided chain. This includes solid circular cylinders `B_2 times (0,L)` and non-ball product domains `B_a times B_b`.
3. Dirichlet annuli may be used as factors on the established Dirichlet side; no unproved Neumann annulus statement is imported.

These are genuine arbitrary-index domain classes, though no literature-novelty claim is made.

## 4. Quantitative localization of a half-Riesz violation

At `gamma=1/2`, subtracting the sharp terms in (1)--(5) gives
\[
 R_{1/2}^D(E)-L_{1/2,d}^{\rm cl}|\Omega|E^{(d+1)/2}
 =\frac12\int_0^E\frac{N_D^{\le}(t)-C_d|\Omega|t^{d/2}}{\sqrt{E-t}}\,dt. \tag{9}
\]
Therefore a Dirichlet half-Riesz excess `delta>0` forces some `t in (0,E)` with
\[
 N_D^{\le}(t)-C_d|\Omega|t^{d/2}\ge\frac{\delta}{\sqrt E}. \tag{10}
\]
The Neumann deficit has the identical conclusion with
`C_d|Omega|t^{d/2}-N_N^<(t)` on the left. This follows because the positive kernel in (9) has total mass `sqrt(E)`.

Thus an actual-domain half-Riesz violation already quantitatively certifies a lower-dimensional Pólya counting violation; the cylinder transfer then propagates it to one higher dimension. A half-Riesz search cannot produce a cylinder counterexample over a base that itself satisfies Pólya. Conversely, product closure shows that bases already covered by Pólya automatically pass the exact half-Riesz test.
