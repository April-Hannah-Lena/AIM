## What an entry means

**Open in cited literature; no later resolution located** means that a scholarly source or specialist problem list identifies the relevant question as unresolved, and a targeted search did not locate a later resolution matching the stated hypotheses. It is an evidence-based catalogue status, not a mathematical certificate that a proof does not exist. Entries explain material restrictions, partial results, and conflicting or historical claims where found.

The date is the date of the literature check, not the publication date of the latest source and not a claim of exhaustive coverage of everything published that day. Search indexing can lag, some full texts are inaccessible, and preprints can change. A source's crawl date was not treated as its publication date. Where only an abstract or introduction was accessible, the entry does not claim a line-by-line review of the whole paper.

## Research procedure

1. Locate a precise conjecture or explicit unresolved question in a paper, book, survey, proceedings article, or expert problem list.
2. Write a self-contained mathematical formulation in original words. Retain conditions such as dimension, boundary regularity, symmetry, coefficient class, and whether a statement is scalar or matrix-valued.
3. Search by the problem name and by its content, including later proofs, counterexamples, and recent publications. Inspect primary sources for promising results. Search topics are recorded on each page; these are concise discovery records rather than an exhaustive search-engine transcript.
4. Compare a candidate resolution with the exact statement. A result on lattices does not automatically treat arbitrary point configurations; a radial theorem does not treat nonradial data; a local or small-data theorem does not give arbitrary-data global regularity.
5. Exclude resolved candidates and candidates with a material unresolved status conflict when a dependable replacement is available. Keep a short exclusion record with sources. When an older claim is contradicted by later expert literature treating the problem as open, explain the distinction rather than silently ignoring it.
6. Audit the statements and metadata across subject groups, and validate the catalogue and local navigation mechanically.

No attempt was made to solve the included problems or to validate every proof in their bibliographies. Automatic checks cannot establish novelty, mathematical independence, correctness of a conjecture, or completeness of a literature search. Before choosing an entry as a research project, repeat its status search and consult a specialist.

## Source access and attribution

Bibliographies link to author manuscripts, arXiv records, publishers, institutional repositories, and expert problem lists. Mathematical statements are rewritten and scoped; books and publications are not reproduced wholesale. A reference may provide the original question, a later status assessment, a known partial result, or explanatory background; its role is described when it affects interpretation.

The [source maps](README.md) record the main books and specific paper locators used for each expansion. They distinguish published books from evolving author manuscripts and lecture notes. In particular, a recently updated manuscript is a source for its stated open questions, not an independent verification of every historical statement it contains.

The exclusion notes are research records, not part of the active catalogue count. They include both resolved problems and candidates omitted because the available claims required further adjudication; those reasons are kept distinct.
